Signs of a hidden leak

Most hidden leaks announce themselves in small ways. A water bill that climbs with no change in habits is the most common sign. Others include a hissing or running sound when every fixture is off, warm spots on a floor, musty smell in a closet, bubbling paint, or a stain that grows after rain-free days.

The EPA's WaterSense program says household leaks waste nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year in an average home, and that roughly 10 percent of homes lose 90 gallons or more a day. That is why a quiet leak is worth a visit even when nothing looks wet.

When a leak is an emergency

Water coming through a ceiling, soaking a wall or pooling under a slab edge needs same-day attention. Close the main valve, switch off power to wet areas only if you can do it safely, and call. We handle leak emergencies at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

If the leak is slow but your meter is turning, it is still worth calling soon. Water that runs behind a wall for weeks feeds mold, and the repair grows with every day of delay.

How we find a leak without opening the wall

We begin with a meter test. With everything off, a moving meter dial means a leak somewhere on the property. Isolating the house from the service line tells us whether the problem is inside or in the yard.

Then we narrow it down:

  • Acoustic listening with ground and pipe microphones picks up the sound of escaping water.
  • Thermal imaging shows temperature changes where hot lines leak behind a surface.
  • Pressure testing isolates one section of line at a time.
  • A camera inspection checks drain and sewer lines when the leak is waste water.
