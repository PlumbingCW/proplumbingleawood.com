Mission Hills: a neighborhood-first look

The city says Mission Hills was envisioned in 1912 by planner J.C. Nichols, and its own website describes stately homes and landscaped gardens, with three golf courses forming a green belt around the residential area. Houses here are large, many are old, and the grounds are mature.

For plumbing, that means three things. Long pipe runs across big floor plans make hidden leaks harder to find. Original supply and drain materials may still be in the walls of homes from the early decades. And mature trees and large lots put long sewer service lines under roots. Each one favors careful diagnosis over quick guesses.

Hidden leaks in big houses

In a house with many bathrooms and long runs, a slow leak can go unnoticed for weeks. A rising water bill or a damp corner is the first clue. Leak detection uses acoustic and thermal tools to find the spot, which matters when finished walls are expensive to open. If the house has original pipes, repiping is the longer-term answer.

Large homes often have multiple water heaters, and one failing unit can flood a finished basement. A water heater leak is urgent, see water heater replacement.
