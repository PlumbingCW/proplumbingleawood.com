Planned replacement beats emergency replacement

A tank water heater has a limited life, and the signs of age are usually visible before it fails. Look for rust at the base, moisture on the floor, popping noises or water that runs lukewarm. If you are seeing those signs, a planned replacement lets you choose the size and type instead of taking whatever is available after a leak.

If the tank is still working, water heater repair can handle a failed element, thermostat or valve. If you are considering a tankless unit, we check the gas line, venting and water pressure first.

Hard water and what it does to a home

WaterOne has published a hardness goal of 120 to 140 mg/L for its treated water, which sits at the line between moderately hard and hard. That figure comes from an older WaterOne publication, so check its current annual water quality report. Hardness leaves scale in tanks, aerators and shower heads over the years.

If you see white crust on fixtures or your heater runs noisily, water softening and filtration is worth a look. A yearly tank flush also helps.

Updated bathrooms and kitchens

Many homes in a neighborhood this age have had bathrooms or kitchens redone at least once. Updated rooms can sit on the original supply lines and drains, so a new faucet or toilet is only as good as the pipes behind it. We can handle faucet repair, toilet repair and installation and the shutoff valves under them, and note anything behind the wall that looks worn.

Sump pumps and drain rules

If your basement has a sump pit, test it before the wet season. Johnson County Wastewater says discharging a sump pump into the sanitary sewer is against code and that its inflow program deals with it. The pump should discharge outside, away from the foundation. We cover sump pump installation and repair.
