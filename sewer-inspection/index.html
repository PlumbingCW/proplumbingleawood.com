Sewer Camera Inspection in Leawood, KS

A sewer camera inspection shows the inside of your drain line on video, so you know whether you have a clog, a crack or a collapsed section before you spend money. We inspect lines in Leawood, KS, and the 24/7 line is open if a backup cannot wait.

What a sewer camera inspection shows

A waterproof camera on a flexible rod travels through the line while a monitor shows the pipe wall. We see grease buildup, roots, cracks, offset joints, bellies where the pipe sags, and the point where a private line meets the public main. A locator at the surface marks the depth and position of any problem. Homeowners on Leawood's older streets can read how this applies on our Old Leawood page.

The video gives you evidence for repair decisions and for conversations with insurers, sellers and neighbors. You see the same pipe we do.

When to schedule one

  • Before buying a home, especially an older one with its original drain.
  • After a repeat backup that cleaning did not fix.
  • When you hear gurgling or smell sewer gas with no obvious source.
  • Before landscaping, a remodel or tree removal near the line.
  • After a repair, to confirm the work.

Planned inspections are scheduled at a time that suits you, and the 24/7 line is open if a backup turns into an emergency. We book same-day appointments when the schedule allows.

Why older Leawood lines deserve a look

Pipe material depends on the home's age. Clay and cast iron were common through the mid-1900s, while PVC took over later. Neither type is a problem on its own, and age does not always mean failure. An inspection is how you find out, and it costs far less than digging blind.

Johnson County Wastewater says homeowners are responsible for the private service line to the public main, so the inspection covers the part you own. It also runs a voluntary inflow program for sump pumps, downspouts and cleanouts connected to the sanitary sewer, which is separate from this work.

Reading the video together

The camera feed shows features that look alike to a beginner. A joint where two pipes meet looks like a ring, and a crack looks like a line. A belly shows as a section where water pools and the camera view is half submerged. Roots appear as a white mat of fine hair or thick brown masses at a joint.

We pause on each finding, mark its distance from the cleanout and explain what it means. A location marker at the surface gives the depth and position, which matters if digging is needed. You keep the recording and the notes, so a second opinion is easy to get.

Inspections for buyers and sellers

A buyer who wants to know what a seller's drain looks like can schedule an inspection before closing. Sellers can do the same, so that a surprise does not derail a sale. The inspection documents the line on a given day, which is a record both sides can use. If problems appear, the video helps you negotiate a repair or a credit.

Keep in mind that an inspection covers the line it can reach. A line blocked by heavy buildup or a collapse may need cleaning or repair before the camera passes.

What the utility says about your line

According to Johnson County Wastewater, the homeowner maintains the private sewer line from the house to the public main. A camera record helps you decide where responsibility sits before you buy or repair.
