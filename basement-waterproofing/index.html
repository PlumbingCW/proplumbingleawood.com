Where basement water comes from

Water enters a basement for a few common reasons. Surface water flows toward the foundation because the grade slopes the wrong way or a downspout empties near the wall. Groundwater builds against the footing and pushes through cracks or the joint between floor and wall. Plumbing leaks and sewer backups can also wet a basement, and they need a different fix.

The pattern tells us which. Water that appears a day after heavy rain suggests groundwater pressure. Water within minutes suggests surface flow. Water with no rain suggests a plumbing leak, so start with leak detection.

What we do about it

Interior drainage and a sump pump are the plumbing side of the fix. A perimeter drain along the footing collects water and sends it to a sump pit, where a pump discharges it away from the house. This handles groundwater pressure without digging outside the foundation.

Outside fixes help just as much. Extend downspouts several feet from the wall, regrade soil so it slopes away, and clear window wells. Those cost little and reduce the load on any pump. We coordinate with other trades for work outside plumbing.

Local rules to know

Johnson County Wastewater states that connecting a sump pump discharge to the sanitary sewer is against code, so drain tile and pump discharge must go to the yard, not into the sewer. Its JoCo Inflow program evaluates private sources of stormwater entering the sanitary sewer, and it pays for approved repairs. If your basement has ever backed up with water during heavy rain, an evaluation is worth requesting.

Storm sewer problems, such as street drains, are a city matter rather than a Johnson County Wastewater matter. Contact your city hall for those.

Humidity versus water

Not every damp basement has a water problem. In summer, warm moist air meets cool walls and condenses, so the surface looks wet when no water is entering. A dehumidifier and good air circulation often fix that. A simple test is to tape a square of plastic to the wall for a day. Moisture under the plastic means water is coming through the wall. Moisture on top means condensation.

Matching the fix to the cause saves money. Drains and pumps handle groundwater, and a dehumidifier handles humidity.

Rules that affect a wet basement

According to Johnson County Wastewater, a sump pump may not discharge to the sanitary sewer, and its JoCo Inflow program looks at private sources of stormwater. Those guidelines shape where a drain or pump can send water.
