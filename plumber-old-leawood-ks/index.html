What we can say about Old Leawood

A Clio local history entry says Leawood was incorporated in 1948 and named for Oscar G. Lee, who bought a large tract in the 1920s. The same entry uses the name Old Leawood for the older part of the city north of I-435, where lots and homes were put in place between 1948 and the 1960s. It also lists Ranch Mart, at Mission Road and 95th Street, among the first shopping centers in Johnson County.

Why we do not guess in an older house

Houses built over many decades often contain a mix of materials from different eras. Some pipes may be original, some replaced, and some tied into the old ones by a past owner. A quote written from the outside of the house only guesses at that mix.

That is why our first step is a look. A sewer camera inspection shows the main drain from the cleanout to the street. A leak detection visit finds a wet spot without opening a wall that may have plaster or old tile behind it.

Sewer lines and mature trees

Established streets usually have established trees, and roots seek water. If you have repeated backups in the lowest drain, a camera shows whether roots, a sag or a broken joint is the cause. Roots in a clean line can often be cleared with drain cleaning, and a damaged section may need sewer line repair.

Johnson County Wastewater says homeowners maintain, repair and replace the private sewer service line that runs to the main. In practical terms, a failure between your house and the street is your repair, so it pays to know the line's condition before it fails.

When a remodel or older supply lines are the question

If your faucets sputter, pressure is uneven or you see stains on a ceiling, the cause may be a supply line, a fitting or a slow leak. We test pressure first, then look at the visible pipe. If the evidence points to widespread wear, we explain the choices, including a partial or whole-house repipe, and what each would involve.
