Water Softener and Filtration in Leawood, KS

Hard water leaves scale on fixtures, spots on glass and buildup in water heaters. Softeners and filters address different problems, so the right system starts with a water test. We install and service both in Leawood, KS, and the 24/7 line is open for plumbing emergencies.

Test your tap first

The right system depends on what is in your water. A home test kit or a lab test gives you hardness, and a more detailed test covers other things. Do not buy a system on a sales pitch before you know your water.

WaterOne supplies Leawood, and it states on its FAQ that water hardness is considered an aesthetic issue. It has published a hardness goal of 120 to 140 mg/L, an older figure, and its annual water quality report holds the current numbers. To convert ppm to grains per gallon, WaterOne says to divide by 17.1.

What WaterOne says about softeners

WaterOne calls a softener a personal choice. It says softeners may reduce soap and detergent use, but that home plumbing becomes more susceptible to corrosion, and that softened water increases sodium intake. It also says a softener should not be paired with a whole-house carbon filter, since carbon removes the beneficial chlorine at the incoming supply.

That is the honest tradeoff. A softener protects water heaters and fixtures from scale, while softened water can be harder on some plumbing. We talk through it with you, not around it.

A reverse osmosis system under the kitchen sink gives drinking-quality water at one tap. A softener treats the whole house. Pair them with an eye on the carbon note above.

Installation and service

A softener installs on the main line after the shutoff, with a drain connection and an electrical outlet. A bypass valve lets you shut it out of service. Salt-based systems need regular salt and a brine tank clean. We service existing systems that have stopped regenerating or leaked. Owners of larger homes can see how this fits on our Hallbrook page.

If your goal is a longer life for appliances, see water heater installation and tankless water heater service, since both care about scale.

Questions to ask before you buy any system

Ask what the system removes, and ask for the test that shows it. Ask what it costs to run, including salt, filters, water used for regeneration and electricity. Ask who services it and how long the warranty lasts. Ask where the drain line goes, since a softener discharges brine during regeneration.

Be cautious about door-to-door water tests and offers that rely on pressure. A free test that ends in a large contract deserves a second opinion. A test you run yourself, or one from a lab, is better evidence than a demonstration that turns a sample brown.

Maintaining the system you choose

Salt-based softeners need salt in the brine tank, and a hard crust, called a salt bridge, can form and stop regeneration. Break it up and keep the level above the water line. Carbon and sediment filters need cartridge changes on a schedule, usually every few months to a year depending on use. An overdue filter slows flow and stops doing its job.

How hardness is measured

According to WaterOne, you divide hardness in ppm by 17.1 to get grains per gallon. Use that guideline with a test of your own tap to size a softener, and ask for a filter that is certified to NSF/ANSI standards for the contaminant you care about.
