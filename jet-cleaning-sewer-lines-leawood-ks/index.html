Jet Cleaning Sewer Lines in Leawood, KS: When It Is Worth It

Jet cleaning a sewer line is worth it when the same line keeps clogging after a cable, when grease or scale lines the pipe, or when roots return every season. It is not the answer for a cracked or collapsed pipe, and a camera tells you which you have.

By the Leawood Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 12, 2026

Cable versus jet in plain terms

A drain cable, or snake, pushes a spinning head through a blockage and cuts a channel. Water can flow through the hole, though the walls stay coated. A jet sends high-pressure water out of a nozzle that scrubs the whole pipe interior and flushes debris downstream.

Think of the difference as poking a hole through a clogged straw versus rinsing the straw clean. For a one-time clog, the cable is enough. For buildup, the jet removes what the cable leaves behind.

Signs that jetting makes sense

  • A line that backs up again within weeks or months of a cable cleaning.
  • Grease buildup in a kitchen line or a restaurant drain.
  • Scale on old cast iron that narrows the pipe.
  • Root hair that returns after cutting.
  • A camera image showing coated walls and a mostly intact pipe.

If your problem looks like a single hair clog in a bathroom sink, jetting is overkill. See drain cleaning for that.

When not to jet

High pressure can worsen a damaged pipe. A cracked, offset, belly-ridden or collapsed line may need repair rather than cleaning. A jetter can also flood a house if a line is blocked at a spot where water has nowhere to go and the nozzle pushes it backward. That is why we run a camera first on lines we have not seen.

Old clay or brittle cast iron needs particular care. The camera shows the condition, and we choose a pressure setting that matches it.

What the process looks like

  • Locate the cleanout, usually a capped pipe in the basement, crawl space or yard.
  • Run a camera to see the line and note any defects.
  • Feed the jetting hose and nozzle, starting with lower pressure.
  • Clean the line back and forth until the water returns clear.
  • Camera the line again to confirm the walls are clean.
  • Review what we saw and explain whether a repair is needed.

A residential jetting visit usually takes a couple of hours. The after-camera step is worth asking for, since it proves the work.

Residential and commercial uses

Homes with persistent root trouble or old cast iron get the most from a jetting visit. Restaurants and shops along busy retail areas such as Roe Avenue and Nall Avenue put heavy loads on their lines, and scheduled jetting keeps grease from becoming a Saturday-night backup. See commercial plumbing for after-hours work.

What to expect when you hire a jetting service

A professional visit should include a camera look, the jetting itself, and a follow-up camera pass. You should receive an explanation of what was found and what was done. If there is a problem that cleaning cannot fix, such as a crack or a belly, the technician should show you on video.

Ask about the equipment. A jetter that produces enough flow and the right nozzle for your pipe diameter cleans better than a small unit. Ask whether the technician will adjust pressure for older pipe. And ask what it costs if the line proves to be damaged and needs repair rather than cleaning, so the budget is clear.

How often to repeat it

After a jetting, the line is clean, and buildup returns at the rate that grease, scale and roots allow. A home with ordinary use may never need another jetting. A restaurant may need a schedule. If a line clogs again within months of a jetting, that is a clue that something structural is happening, and a camera look is the next step.

Local note: who owns which part of the sewer

Johnson County Wastewater says its collection system includes more than 2,300 miles of sewer mains and six treatment facilities, including the Tomahawk Creek facility in east central Johnson County. The utility states that homeowners are responsible for the private service line from the house to the public main. If your jetting job is on your private line, the decision is yours, and if neighbors are backing up too, the public main may be the issue.

Johnson County Wastewater also runs a 24/7 line for suspected sewer backups, listed on its website. If you are in Overland Park or Prairie Village, the same utility rules apply, and a camera inspection shows which side of the property line the trouble is on.
