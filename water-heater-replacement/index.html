When a failed water heater is an emergency

A tank leaking from its body cannot be repaired, and it will get worse. If the floor is wet, the unit is rusted at the base or water runs from beneath the jacket, replacement is the answer. A cold house in January makes it more urgent.

We handle replacement emergencies at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. Shutting off the cold supply and the fuel buys you time and stops the damage from growing.

What replacement involves

We drain the old tank, disconnect the gas or electric supply, and haul the unit away. The new heater is set, connected to new flexible lines, and fitted with a temperature and pressure relief valve and a discharge line. On gas units, we check the vent for draft and the gas connection for leaks before we light it. For a home in a neighborhood developed in the 1970s and 1980s, see our Leawood South page.

Many homes also need an expansion tank when the water system is closed, because heated water expands and can drive pressure up. We check the thermal expansion setup and tell you if it needs updating. Ask the Leawood building department about permit requirements, since rules vary.

Choosing a replacement: size, fuel and type

Match tank size to household demand. A 40-gallon unit often suits two or three people, while a family of four or five tends to use a 50-gallon or larger tank. Gas recovers faster than electric, and a heat pump unit uses less electricity but needs space and airflow.

You can also move to a tankless unit at replacement time. It saves floor space and heats on demand, though it costs more up front and may need a larger gas line. If you are planning rather than reacting, see water heater installation.

Safety checks we expect on every swap

A replacement is a chance to bring an old setup up to current practice. The relief valve and its discharge pipe should be new or tested, the gas connection should be leak checked, and the vent should slope and seal properly. Gas tank models follow ANSI Z21.10.1. Ask which items the quote covers, and ask the Leawood building department whether your job needs a permit.

Standards a replacement should meet

Gas tank water heaters follow ANSI Z21.10.1, and the manual lists the vent, clearance and relief valve guidelines. A good replacement meets all of them.
