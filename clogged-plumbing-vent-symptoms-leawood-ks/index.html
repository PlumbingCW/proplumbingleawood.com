Clogged Plumbing Vent Symptoms: A Leawood, KS Homeowner Guide

A clogged plumbing vent makes drains gurgle, flush slowly and smell like sewer, because the pipe cannot let air in behind moving water. The vent is the pipe that runs up through your roof, and it is often the last place anyone looks.

By the Leawood Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 12, 2026

What a plumbing vent does

Every drain needs air. When water flows down a pipe, it pushes air ahead and pulls air behind. The vent pipe, usually rising through the roof, lets air in so water moves smoothly and the trap under each fixture keeps its seal. Without that air, drains slow and traps empty.

A trap is the curved section of pipe that holds a little water as a barrier against sewer gas. If the vent fails, the trap can siphon dry and let gas into the house.

Common symptoms of a blocked vent

  • Gurgling from a sink, tub or toilet when another fixture drains.
  • Slow drains in several fixtures that a snake does not fix.
  • A toilet that flushes weakly or bubbles after a flush.
  • A sewer smell near drains or in a bathroom.
  • Water that drains in bursts instead of smoothly.
  • A trap that has lost its water seal.

One slow drain is usually a clog in that fixture's own pipe. Several symptoms across the house, or symptoms that appear only when another fixture runs, point to the vent or the main drain.

What blocks a vent

Vents are open to the sky, and things fall in. Leaves, nests, snow and ice can block the opening. A bird nest in the stack, a dead rodent or a tennis ball that landed on the roof can all stop flow. Inside the house, a vent can be crushed or disconnected during a remodel. In freezing weather, frost can narrow the opening, since moist air freezes at the top.

Check it safely

  • Look at the vent opening from the ground with binoculars if you can see it.
  • Do not climb onto a roof in rain, ice or wind.
  • Listen for gurgling when you run a bath or flush upstairs.
  • Note which fixtures are affected and write it down.
  • Call a plumber if you need roof access, because the fall risk is real.

A plumber clears a vent from the roof with a cable or hose, or from inside the attic when the stack is reachable. We can also camera the line to separate a vent problem from a drain problem. That matters because a vent blocked by a nest needs a different fix than a clog in the main line. For the main line, see sewer line cleaning.

When a gurgle means the main line

Gurgling in the lowest drain, along with a toilet that backs up when you run the washer, suggests the main sewer line is partly blocked. In that case, the water has nowhere to go, and air is forced back up through traps. The vent is fine and the line is not. A camera inspection sorts it out.

How a plumber diagnoses it

A technician starts with the symptoms and the pattern. Which fixtures gurgle, and when? Does flushing the toilet affect the sink? Does it happen only in wind, in cold weather or after rain? Those clues point to the vent or the drain.

Then comes a test. A camera on a cable can travel down a clean-out to see the main line, while a smoke or water test can trace a vent. If the main line is clear, the vent becomes the suspect, and a cable or hose from the roof clears it. The key is separating a drain clog from a vent block, because the fixes are different.

Prevention and what not to do

Keep the vent opening clear of branches and debris, and have it checked after a big storm. Do not cap a vent or cover it, since it needs open air. Do not tie a new fixture into an existing drain without a vent plan. A remodel that adds a fixture without venting it is one of the most common causes of gurgling in a home that had no trouble before.

What plumbing codes require of vents

Model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) require traps to be vented so that water can leave the fixture smoothly. That is why a blocked vent behaves like a clog.

Local note: odors, gas and who to call

Johnson County Wastewater lists help diagnosing persistent sewer odors on its website. If you smell rotten eggs, though, treat it as gas, leave the home and call your gas provider or 911 first, which is also the utility's guidance. Sewer gas smells different, more like waste, but when you are unsure, leave and call.

Permit and code rules for vent work vary by city, so ask the Leawood building department before you change the roof penetration. For homes in Prairie Village or Overland Park, ask the building office in your city.
