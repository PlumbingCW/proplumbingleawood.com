Copper Repipe Cost: A Leawood, KS Price Guide

The cost of a copper repipe depends on the size of the house, how many fixtures it has and how hard the walls are to open. Copper costs more than PEX for materials and labor, and it also has a long record. Here is how to read a quote.

By the Leawood Pro Plumbing team · Updated November 12, 2026

What sets the price

A repipe quote is built from a handful of factors. We do not list dollar figures here, because the right number depends on your house, and an honest price comes from a visit.

  • Square footage and number of floors.
  • Number of bathrooms, kitchens and laundry hookups.
  • Pipe material, copper type and fittings.
  • Wall, ceiling and slab access.
  • Drywall patching and finish work.
  • Permits and inspection.
  • Whether the water service line is included.

Houses with many bathrooms cost more because each adds fixtures and runs. Two-story homes can be cheaper to route than a sprawling one-story with a slab.

Copper compared with PEX

Copper versus PEX for repiping
FactorCopperPEX
Material costHigherLower
LaborMore time, solderingFaster, flexible runs
Freeze toleranceSplitsExpands and often survives
LongevityDecadesDecades, newer record
Water chemistryCan corrode in some waterNot subject to corrosion
Fire and heatNot affectedNeeds protection from heat

Copper has a long track record and handles high temperatures. PEX is less costly and quicker to install. Neither is a bad choice, and the right one depends on your budget, your house and your water.

What a good quote includes

A good quote states the material, the scope and the exclusions. It says how many fixtures it covers, whether the water service line is included, who patches drywall and when payment is due. Ask whether the price includes permits, and whether it includes testing and a warranty.

A quote that is much lower than others often leaves out patching, permits or part of the house. Ask what is not in it.

Do you need a full repipe at all?

One leak does not mean the whole house needs repiping. Repair a single failure and watch. If leaks appear in different places within a year, or the water runs brown and pressure falls, a repipe or a section repipe becomes the practical answer. See burst pipe repair for the urgent step, and leak detection to find leaks you cannot see.

Timeline and disruption

A whole-house repipe often takes a few days, with water off during working hours. Walls are opened at fixtures and along runs, and the new lines go in before the old ones are abandoned. We pressure-test before drywall closes. You can usually stay in the home, though kitchens and bathrooms are out of service for part of the time.

Section repipes and phased work

You do not always have to repipe the whole house at once. If the problem is one wing, a bathroom group or the kitchen, a section repipe fixes it and leaves the rest. That spreads the cost and often removes the worst risk first.

Phased work needs a plan. The new section should connect cleanly to the old pipe, using a transition fitting that suits the metals. Mixing copper and galvanized steel directly causes corrosion, so a dielectric fitting is needed. Ask how the installer plans to join old and new, and whether the plan sets you up for the next phase.

Warranty and what it should cover

A repipe warranty should cover workmanship, fittings and leaks that come from the installation. Ask how long it lasts and what voids it. The pipe itself also has a manufacturer's warranty. Keep copies of both, along with a diagram of the new pipe runs if the installer provides one, since it helps with future repairs and with a home sale.

Signs it is time to repipe

A few patterns suggest repiping beats continued repairs. Pinhole leaks in copper that show up in several rooms, blue green stains on pipes, water that runs brown after sitting and falling pressure on every floor all point to failing pipe. Galvanized steel and polybutylene are common candidates because they age badly.

Keep a log of each repair with the date and the cost. When repair bills for a year start to approach a meaningful share of a repipe quote, the comparison is easier to see. A written log also gives a plumber and your insurer a clear history.

Safety steps and standards to ask about

Copper tube for plumbing is made to ASTM B88, and PEX is made to ASTM F876. Soldering involves open flame inside walls, so ask the installer how they protect framing and what fire precautions they use.

  • Ask which ASTM standard the pipe meets.
  • Ask how the crew protects framing during soldering.
  • Keep pets and children out of work areas.
  • Confirm that the water stays off at night or is restored safely.

Local note: what WaterOne says about service lines

WaterOne, which supplies Leawood, states that it has no record of lead utility service lines in its distribution system, and that the customer-owned section of the service line is the customer's responsibility. That means a repipe in Leawood is mostly about the pipes inside your house, plus possibly the customer-owned service section. Ask your plumber whether the quote includes that section, since it may be a separate cost.

Hardness shapes pipe life too. In an older document, WaterOne described a hardness goal of 120 to 140 mg/L, and mineral deposits build on pipe interiors over many years. Check its current annual report. If you are in Prairie Village or Mission Hills, older homes are the usual candidates for a repipe.
