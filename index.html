Your first five minutes: stop the damage before we arrive

These four moves limit the damage whether the problem is a burst pipe, an overflowing toilet or a failed water heater. They cost nothing and take minutes.

1

Shut off the water

Turn the main shutoff valve clockwise until it stops. It is usually where the water line enters the house, often near the water heater or in the basement. Test it once a year so it is not stuck on the day you need it.

2

Mind the electricity

If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, switch off power at the breaker only if the floor around the panel is dry. If it is not dry, stay out and say so when you call.

3

Stop using drains

When a toilet or tub backs up, stop flushing and stop running water. Every fixture feeds the same blocked line, so each use pushes more into the house.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell us what is wet, what you shut off and where you are. If you smell gas, leave the home and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Plumbing problems we fix across Leawood

Water where it should not be is the most urgent call. A burst pipe can flood a room in minutes. A hidden supply leak needs leak detection before anyone cuts into drywall. A failing line from the street may point to water service line repair, and a sump pump that quits in a storm calls for sump pump repair.

Backups come next. A slow tub or a gurgling toilet is often a plain drain cleaning job. When several fixtures back up together, the main line is the suspect, and the fix is sewer line cleaning, hydro jetting for grease and roots, or a camera inspection to see what is down there. A cracked or collapsed pipe needs sewer line repair.

Hot water is third. A tank that leaks from the bottom usually means water heater replacement, while a dead pilot or a burned-out element may only need water heater repair. Planned work such as water heater installation or a tankless water heater can be booked for a time that suits you.

Fixtures and upgrades round out the list: toilets, faucets, garbage disposals, new plumbing installation, repiping for old galvanized or leaking pipe, basement waterproofing and water softener and filtration. Businesses can call about commercial plumbing after hours as well.

What Leawood homeowners should know about their water and sewer lines

WaterOne supplies drinking water to Leawood along with 16 other cities, and it states that the customer-owned section of the water service line is the customer's responsibility to repair, replace or maintain. A leak in that stretch is yours to fix, and the first call is to a plumber, not the utility.

WaterOne also says it has no record of lead utility service lines in its distribution system. That is one less worry when you plan a repipe, because the work is about the pipe inside the house rather than the street side.

WaterOne has published a hardness goal of 120 to 140 mg/L for treated water, right at the line between moderately hard and hard on the USGS scale. That figure comes from an older WaterOne publication, so check its current annual water quality report for today's number. Either way, scale builds up in tank water heaters over the years, which is why a yearly flush and a look at the anode rod matter, see water heater repair.
