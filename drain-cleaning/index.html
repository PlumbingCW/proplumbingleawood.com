When a clogged drain is an emergency

One slow sink can wait until morning. A toilet that will not flush, a tub that fills with gray water when the washer drains, or sewage showing at a basement floor drain cannot. Those signs mean the blockage is deep, and every flush or load of laundry pushes more water toward the lowest opening in the house.

If that is your situation, stop using water, move valuables off the floor and call. We handle drain emergencies at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

How we clear a drain: snaking first, then stronger tools

Most household clogs sit in the first 25 to 40 feet of pipe, where hair, soap scum, grease and paper collect at a bend or a trap. A hand auger or a motorized drain snake with a cutting head breaks that mass loose and pulls it out. For a bathroom sink or tub, the job is often finished in one visit.

When the snake keeps hitting the same spot or the water drains slowly again within days, the cause is usually not a clog at all. It may be a belly in the pipe that holds water, a root intrusion or a cracked joint. In that case we step up to hydro jetting or a camera inspection so the repair matches what is really down there.

Why drains clog in Leawood homes

Leawood homes come from several eras, and pipe material changes what clogs look like. Older cast iron drains develop rough, scaled interiors that catch grease and paper. Newer PVC stays smooth but still collects hair and soap at bends. Mature trees in established neighborhoods send roots toward the moisture inside old clay pipe or cracked joints.

The practical point is simple: a clog that returns every few months in the same fixture is telling you something about the pipe. Pay for the camera look once rather than the snake call five times. Please keep chemical drain openers out of the pipes, since Johnson County's Environmental Division handles household chemical disposal and those products can damage old pipe.

Safety steps around clogs

Do not mix chemical drain cleaners, and never use them before a plumber visit, since the liquid can burn skin and damage pipe. Wear gloves and eye protection near a trap, and keep children and pets away. If water is backing up from the lowest drain in the house, stop using water and call. Plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) require traps and vents that a clog can reveal.

  • Stop running water when a drain backs up.
  • Keep chemical cleaners away from pipes a plumber will open.
  • Wash your hands after touching drain water.
  • Call right away if sewage reaches the floor.
