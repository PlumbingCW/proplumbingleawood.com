Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Leawood, KS

The water service line carries drinking water from the meter to your house, and when it breaks you get a flooded yard, a basement wall that seeps or pressure that drops for the whole house. Our 24/7 line is open now for Leawood, KS. Close the main valve inside the house and call.

Signs of a failing water service line

A service line is buried, so the signs show up elsewhere. Look for a wet or soft patch in the yard with no rain, or grass that is greener or sinking along the line's path. Other signs are a water meter that keeps turning with the house off, a drop in pressure at every tap and a sudden jump in the water bill.

Water in a basement wall at the point where the line enters the house is another classic sign. Pressure that fades over weeks points to corrosion or a slow leak, while a sudden loss points to a break.

When it is an emergency

A broken line can lose hundreds of gallons an hour and undermine soil around a foundation. Shut the main valve inside the house, and if you can find the utility's valve at the meter, leave it for the utility. We take service line emergencies at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Before any digging, call 811 through Kansas One Call so buried lines are marked. The utility does not mark private lines.

Who is responsible for what in Leawood

WaterOne supplies Leawood and states that the customer-owned section of the service line is the customer's responsibility to replace, repair or maintain. That means a break between the meter and your house is yours to fix, and a plumber should locate it first.

WaterOne also says it has no record of lead utility service lines in its distribution system. That is good news when you plan replacement, since the question is usually the pipe material of your own line, such as copper or old galvanized steel.

Planning the dig

Before digging, we call 811 so buried utilities are marked, and we note the route of the old line and any trees, patios or driveways above it. The new line goes in with a bedding of sand or fine soil, and the trench is backfilled in layers. We pressure-test the new line before covering it, then restore what we can.
