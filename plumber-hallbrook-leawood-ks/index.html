Listing sites describe the neighborhood as mostly custom and estate-scale single-family homes, and place it in ZIP codes 66211 and 66206. Many fixtures, many demands

A house with several bathrooms, a large kitchen and perhaps a laundry on each floor can ask a lot of the water supply at once. Showers running together, a dishwasher and a washing machine can drop pressure or push a single water heater past what it can supply. The fix depends on the house, and sizing is the first thing we check.

If the household regularly runs out of hot water, a new or additional water heater or a tankless setup may be the answer. We measure demand before we recommend one.

Water quality and whole-house filtration

WaterOne, which serves Leawood, states that the customer-owned section of the water service line is the customer's responsibility. Inside the house, what you notice is usually scale, taste or staining. Water softening and filtration can address scale and some of those complaints, and a water service line check covers the pipe that brings water into the house.

Leaks in a big house

The longer the pipe runs, the more places a slow leak can hide. A rising water bill or a damp spot on a ceiling is the first clue. Leak detection helps us find the spot before finished walls are opened. For a burst pipe or active flow, shut the main valve first and call.
