When a sump pump failure is an emergency

Sump pumps work hardest when it matters most. If the pit fills during heavy rain and the pump does not start, water can reach the floor in an hour. We handle sump pump emergencies at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

Check the plug and breaker first, then lift the float to see if the pump starts. A pump that runs but moves no water may have a blocked intake or a failed check valve.

Why sump pumps fail

  • Float switch jammed against the pit wall.
  • Motor burned out from age, often after 7 to 10 years.
  • Clogged intake screen or impeller.
  • Failed check valve, which lets water flow back into the pit.
  • Power outage with no battery backup.
  • A pump that is undersized for the inflow.

A pump that cycles constantly is also a problem. It may sit in a pit that is too small or have a stuck float.

Backup systems

A battery backup pump takes over when the power fails or the primary pump dies. Storms that bring heavy rain often bring outages too, and that is exactly when a single pump fails. A water-powered backup uses municipal pressure instead of a battery, though it depends on supply pressure and uses water.

Where the water should go

Johnson County Wastewater states that connecting a sump pump discharge to the sanitary sewer is against code, since it can cause backups in your basement or a neighbor's. Discharge should go outdoors, away from the foundation. Its JoCo Inflow program evaluates sump pumps, downspouts, area drains and uncapped cleanouts, and says the program is voluntary and that it pays for approved repairs.

If your pump is connected to a sanitary line, the fix is a rerouted discharge. See sewer line repair when the sanitary line is also stressed.

Installation and replacement

A new pump goes into a properly sized pit with a check valve, a sealed lid and a discharge line that runs away from the house. We choose between submersible and pedestal models based on the pit. If water keeps coming back, basement waterproofing addresses the source.

Sizing the pump and the pit

A pump that is too small cannot keep up in a heavy storm, and one that is too large cycles on and off rapidly and wears out. Horsepower and the height the water must be lifted decide the rating. Most home pumps run between one third and one half horsepower, and larger needs call for a bigger unit or a second pump.

The pit matters as much. A pit that is too narrow forces the float to cycle more often. A properly sized pit has a lid to keep out debris and smell, a stable base and a clear space around the float. When we replace a pump, we check all three.

Standards and local rules for pumps

Sump pumps are electrical equipment, so the circuit should follow the National Electrical Code (NEC), and outlets near water are usually on a GFCI circuit. Johnson County Wastewater states that sump discharge to the sanitary sewer is against code. Ask the Leawood building department whether a new pit or pump needs a permit.
