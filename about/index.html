About Leawood Pro Plumbing

Leawood Pro Plumbing runs one 24/7 phone line for plumbing emergencies and planned work in Leawood, Kansas, and the nearby towns of Overland Park, Prairie Village and Mission Hills.

Call (866) 310-6010 now

What we do

You call one number, and we match your problem with a licensed and insured plumber. The line is open around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays, because plumbing emergencies do not keep office hours.

You hear a clear price before work starts, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. We do not promise arrival times over the phone, since traffic and other emergencies can change them. We tell you what we know as soon as we have your address and the problem.

What we do not do

We do not use forms. Everything happens by phone, so you talk to a person rather than wait for a reply. We do not show reviews, awards or counts we cannot document, and we do not name individual plumbers on this site. This page describes the service and how it works.

How to check any plumber

Whoever you hire, ask for proof of licensing and insurance before work begins. Ask the Leawood building department what permits your project needs, since rules vary by job and by city. Get the price in writing when the job is large, and keep photos of the problem for your insurer.

How a call works

You call the number on this page and a person answers. We ask for your address, what has gone wrong and whether water is still running. If water is spreading, we tell you how to find and close the main shutoff valve before anything else. Then we match the job with a licensed and insured plumber and explain the next step.

Before work starts, you hear the price for the job. If the plumber finds something different once the pipe is open, the price is discussed again before more work begins. You decide whether to go ahead.

What to have ready

A few details make the call shorter and the first visit more useful. Know your street address and ZIP code, and which fixture or pipe has failed. Note whether the problem is hot water, cold water or drain water. If you can safely take a photo of the damage, do it, since your insurer may want one too.

If the main shutoff is stuck or hidden, say so. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Those steps come first, and plumbing waits.

Emergencies and planned work

The line handles both. Emergencies include burst pipes, sewage backing up, a water heater that is leaking, no water at all and a flooded basement. Planned work includes a water heater swap, a new disposal, a faucet or toilet replacement and a camera look at an old sewer line.

Planned work is easier on your budget and your schedule, so do not wait for a failure if a pipe is showing warning signs. Brown water, falling pressure, damp spots and a hot water tank that is more than ten years old are all reasons to call before it becomes an emergency.

Homes and buildings we see

Leawood has newer subdivisions and established neighborhoods, and the age of a house tells us what to expect inside the walls. Older homes may have galvanized steel, cast iron drains or clay sewer laterals. Newer homes mostly have copper or plastic supply pipe and plastic drains. We ask about the age of the house and any past plumbing work so the right parts arrive on the first visit.

Shops, offices and restaurants have their own needs, such as grease lines and after-hours repairs. See commercial plumbing for those jobs.

How we keep this site honest

Every fact about the area on this site comes from a public source, such as WaterOne, Johnson County Wastewater or a city website. Where a rule changes by project or by year, we say to ask the Leawood building department or the utility itself, because a guess from us could cost you money.

We also avoid claims we cannot back up. You will not find invented reviews, ratings or numbers here. If a claim cannot be checked, it stays off the page.
