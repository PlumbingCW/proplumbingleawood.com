Sewer problems that need a repair, not a cleaning

A clog clears with a cable or a jet. A damaged pipe does not. Signs that the pipe itself is the problem include repeated backups after cleaning, a sinkhole or soggy patch in the yard, sewer odor outside, and a camera showing a crack, an offset joint or a section that has collapsed.

We take sewer emergencies at night and on weekends, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. When sewage is inside the house, stop water use, keep children and pets out and call right away.

Why sewer lines fail

Older homes have clay or cast iron drains, and both wear. Clay joints loosen and let roots in. Cast iron corrodes from within. Newer PVC lasts, though ground movement, poor bedding or a collapsed section can still break it.

Heavy rain also exposes weaknesses. Johnson County Wastewater runs a program called JoCo Inflow that evaluates private sources of stormwater entering the sanitary sewer, such as sump pumps, downspouts and uncapped cleanouts. It says the program is voluntary and that it pays for approved repairs. See sump pump repair if a pump is part of your problem.

Who owns what

Johnson County Wastewater states that homeowners are responsible for maintaining, repairing and replacing the private service line, the pipe from the house to the public main. The public main belongs to the utility. Call 811 through Kansas One Call before any digging, since the utility does not locate private lines.

What sewer repair costs: the factors

You get a clear price before work starts. It moves with:

  • Repair method and length of pipe.
  • Depth and what sits above it, such as a driveway or trees.
  • Pipe material and diameter.
  • Whether restoration is needed.
  • Whether it is an after-hours emergency or a scheduled visit.

Restoring the yard and driveway

Repairs that involve digging leave a mark, so ask about restoration before work starts. Sod can be relaid, soil can be backfilled and compacted in layers to avoid settling, and concrete or pavers can be replaced. Trenchless methods avoid most of that damage, which is one reason to consider lining or bursting when your pipe is a fit.

Codes and who signs off

Model codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) set rules for slope, cleanouts and pipe materials. Johnson County Wastewater says the homeowner owns the private line to the public main. Ask the Leawood building department whether a repair needs a permit and an inspection, and ask the utility about any rules at the connection to the main.
